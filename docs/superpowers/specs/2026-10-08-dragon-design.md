# The dragon

Until now the walking dead were the village's only enemy, and every rule about monsters assumed them: they rise at dusk, crumble at dawn and burn a house only while everyone sleeps. The dragon is a second kind of danger, rare and by day. Once in a long while it comes over the hills, sets one to three homes alight, burns whoever it catches in the open, and flies off. Only villagers with a bow, a crossbow or magic can reach it in the air; everyone else runs for cover. It is hard to bring down, but it can be killed.

## What happens

1. **When.** Never before day 8, and never to a village of fewer than six. The first visit falls on a day between 8 and 17; after that one comes every 15 to 30 days. It comes between 10:30 and 16:30. It is put off by an hour while a building burns, while the dead or another dragon fill the monster cap, or when every home is already burning or in ruins; after 16:30 it is put off to the next day. When it is due is worked out from a hash of the world's seed and the day, as loot is, so nothing draws from the RNG until a dragon actually comes and a world that has not seen one runs exactly as it did before.
2. **Arrival.** It appears 150 metres out from the middle of the map, high up (22 metres), and the chronicle says it is coming. It picks one to three homes that still stand, nearest where it came in and lived-in ones first (never more than the fires left under `MAX_FIRES`), and goes for them in the order it reaches them.
3. **The run.** For each home it drops to 7 metres as it closes in, slows, and breathes on the door. The roof catches (at 8 rather than the dead's 20, since it burns from the top and the village is busy hiding), whoever was inside is thrown out burnt as with any house fire, and everyone in the open within 7 metres of the breath is burned for 30 to 50. A healthy adult survives one breath but not two. The chronicle says "The dragon set fire to …", and a death reads "slain by a dragon at …".
4. **Circling.** With its last home alight it hangs over the village for 6 to 10 seconds, between that home and the green, at 10 metres, in a slow circle, and up to 8 seconds longer while someone close below is shooting at it. Every 8 seconds it breathes at someone in the open below: whoever last shot at it if they are close enough, else the nearest. This is when the archers and mages get their best shots, and pay for them.
5. **Leaving.** Then it climbs and flies off over the hills, and the chronicle says how it fared. If it falls below 30% of its health at any point it gives up at once, but labours away at under half its speed, low and slow enough for those below to run after it and finish it.
6. **The village.** Anyone who sees it (from further off than the dead, since it is in the sky) raises the alarm: "A dragon! … Take cover!". A villager with a bow, a crossbow or magic stands or runs from it as they would from the dead. Everyone else, children included, runs for shelter, however brave. The alarm calls out the brave ranged fighters only; the rest stay where they are. The model's "fight" against it from someone with a blade becomes a flight. The bucket chain starts once the hiding is over, which is why a lit home is often lost. Ceremonies are put off while it is about.
7. **Bringing it down.** It has 1200 hit points. Archers shoot from the ground beneath it; range counts its height. When it dies it plays its fall, drops to the ground, and lies there for a minute before it is gone. Whoever landed the last shot gets the kill, a memory and a chronicle line ("… brought down the dragon at …!"), everyone who saw it remembers, and its hoard spills where it fell: a relic and 25 to 35 coins.

## Where it lives

- **The model** (`tools/blender/make_dragon.py`): built from rigid, bevelled parts in flat colours like the props in `make_props.py`, each part bound wholly to one bone of `Rig_Dragon`, with six clips keyed in the script (`Dragon_Fly`, `Dragon_Hover`, `Dragon_Breath`, `Dragon_Roar`, `Dragon_Hit`, `Dragon_Death`). It faces glTF +z like the KayKit characters and stands about 4.7 metres tall with a 9.5-metre wingspan. The script writes `tools/blender/rigged/Dragon.glb` (committed).
- **The asset build** (`tools/assets`): characters and rigs may now be read from the repository (`local: true`), as bundles already could. `Dragon` is a character on the `dragon` rig, whose clips come from the same file. The clips are on no other rig, so the sim (`AnimId::seconds`) and the client (`timeScale`, `animSeconds`) time them by the dragon's own rig, and the seat fix skips a rig that never sits.
- **Data** (`server/crates/sim/src/data/monsters.rs`): `MonsterKind::Dragon`, with weight 0 so it is never in a wave. `MonsterDef` gains `undead` and `flies`. The breath's damage, reach and interval are the def's `damage`, `range` and `interval`.
- **State** (`state.rs`): `Monster.alt` (metres above the ground) and `Monster.raid` (`Raid`: phase, targets, how many are done, where it hovers, where it leaves, the breath under way), `MonsterOrigin::Sky`, `Director.next_dragon_tick` and `Director.alarm_dragon`. All are left out of a save when empty or zero, so older saves load and the fixtures round-trip.
- **The system** (`systems/dragon.rs`): the schedule (`update_schedule`, called by the director once the village is founded), the arrival (`come`), and each tick of the visit (`update_dragon`, called from `update_monsters` for any monster that flies, before anything the dead do). It flies straight, never on the nav grid, and `separate` leaves it out.
- **Fire** (`systems/fire.rs`): `ignite_from_above` lights a home as `ignite_home` does, with its own start and chronicle line.
- **Combat** (`systems/combat.rs`): `can_reach` (no blade reaches a flier) and `reach_dist` (distance counting height). `start_fight` turns a fight nobody can reach into a flight, so perception, the alarm, being hit and the model all go through one gate. `run_fight` paths to the ground below a flier and measures range in three dimensions. `damage_monster` plays the dragon's own hit and death, and `drop_loot` spills its hoard.
- **Perception** (`systems/perception.rs`): it is seen from 1.8 times as far as the dead; the alarm and the stand-down speak of a dragon; only those who can reach it answer the alarm.
- **The brain**: the world text and the conversation rules mention the rare dragon, the planning prompt says a dragon is burning the village while it is, and the react prompt tells a villager with a blade that it is out of reach. `brain/tests/fixtures/prompts.json` was updated for these three phrases.
- **The wire** (`wire.rs`, protocol 10, after the ghosts took 9): `Dyn.y` and `DynDelta.y` (height × 100, absent on the ground), `Ranged::Fire`, `Status.dragon` (the status line counts the dead and the dragon apart), and `MonsterDetail.undead`. The inspector's card says what the dragon is doing ("Swooping on the blue house", "Circling over the village green", "Flying away over the hills").
- **Calling the dragon** (added at the owner's request): `POST /api/admin/dragon`, behind `require_admin`, sends `Command::CallDragon`, and **🛠 Admin → World → Call the dragon** sends that. It sets `Director.dragon_called` (left out of a save when false); the schedule then sends the dragon at the first chance it may come, needing three villagers rather than six, and leaves `next_dragon_tick` as it was. It still waits for its hours, no fire and a home left standing; it is refused (409) while the village is being settled, while the dragon is abroad or when it is already called. Drawing nothing itself, the command keeps a replay of the command stream exact.
- **The client**: `ActorView` lifts an actor by its interpolated height; `Effects` draws the breath as a stream of flame puffs from the mouth to the ground and a ring of fire where it lands; the top bar shows 🐉 while it is about; the inspector calls it "out of the sky"; Help and About describe it. The breath plays the spell sound, louder, its arrival the creature roar the dead rise to, and "A dragon!" rings the alarm bell.

## Measured

Two throwaway harnesses, run in release builds and not kept:

- **One visit, forty times**: summoned on day 9 at hours between 10:30 and 15:30, on seeds 1 to 40 (odd seeds the hand-written cast, even seeds a village the sim invented). About two homes lit per visit, 57% of them lost; one death in five visits; the dragon killed in 45% of visits. A young village of healthy, brave archers is its worst case.
- **A hundred days, ten villages**: seeds 1 to 10 founded as the soak founds them (`FoundVillage`), with and without the dragon. Every village saw four visits. Of 76 homes lit, 50 burned down (none burned in the same hundred days without the dragon; the dead's fires need a whole village asleep, which the watch prevents). The dragon slew 15 villagers in 40 visits and was brought down in 5 of them (one in eight). Deaths in all rose from 122 to 147, the rest of the rise mostly those left sleeping rough by the glade at night, where the dead find them; newcomers kept every village at 12 to 16 living, as without it (13 to 16).

The balance came from four rounds of this. Lingering 15 to 25 seconds over each home it lit lost three homes in four; letting it fly off fast when hurt meant nobody could finish it; a 20-second duel with the archers made it kill one villager a visit and lose 84% of the homes it lit.

`sim/tests/sim.rs`'s 20-day run and the 200-day soak (`--ignored`) keep their bands.

## Left out on purpose

- Sounds of its own: a roar and a breath would need new files from the CC0 packs (`pnpm audio`), so for now it borrows the creature roar and the spell sound.
- More than one dragon at a time, dragons by night, and a lair: it comes and goes over the hills.
- Fire spreading from house to house, as before. Its breath sets homes alight; it does not set the grass or props burning.
- Villagers do not hide from it in particular buildings, prepare for it or remember its last visit beyond their memories and the chronicle.
- The carcass is not a world object: it lies where it fell for a minute and is gone.
