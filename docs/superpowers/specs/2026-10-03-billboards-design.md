# Billboards

Seven large billboards stand around the village. They show funny catch-phrases, mock-motivational slogans and mock adverts, written each day by the language model from what has lately happened (stock slogans without one); later they are meant to carry real adverts too. Villagers sometimes walk up to one, read it, say what they think of it and react to it.

## What was decided

- **Content lives in the sim.** Villagers can only react to text the sim knows, so what a board shows is part of the world. Without a notice, a board shows the day's slogan from a built-in deck of 40, worked out from the seed, the day and the board's index: it draws nothing from the RNG and nothing is saved for it. Text put up from outside (an admin today, an advertiser later) is a `Command::SetBillboard`, applied at a tick boundary and saved in `SimState.billboards`. A future ad feed goes through the same command and the same cleaning.
- **Reactions work without a model.** Every slogan has a tone (uplifting, absurd, pushy, ominous, wholesome). The stock reaction comes from the tone and the reader's temper, bravery and sociability, with no randomness: a hot temper scoffs at uplifting slogans and is annoyed by pushy ones, the timid are unsettled by ominous ones and the brave laugh at them. A stock line for that reaction is said aloud.
- **With a model**, reading a board raises `SimEvent::BillboardRead`; the brain asks `react_to_billboard` (a reaction and an optional line) when it has room to spare (`Tier::Idle`, not backlogged). The reader stands at the board for up to the model's usual wait (`script_wait_ticks`, capped at 30 s of ticks); an answer in time replaces the stock reaction, otherwise the stock one comes at the end. The prompt frames the text as paint on wood, which cannot ask anything of the reader.
- **Who reads, and when.** Only the routine sends people to boards (`ActivityId::ReadBoard` is not offered to the model, so the prompts' activity lists are unchanged). When someone with nothing pressing picks their next thing, a board they have not read within 60 m (in a leisure block) or 20 m (otherwise) with a free reading spot gives a 10% or 3% chance of going to read it. Each villager remembers a hash of the last text read at each board and never reads the same text there twice. Over five days on three seeds that is about 15 reads a day for 12 villagers.
- **Boards are taken to heart.** What a board says can urge something (`Advice` in `data/billboards.rs`): an activity (pray, eat, drink at the tavern, rest, which is done at home, fish, shop, train, mine, forage, visit the graves), the reader's own work, or a friendly word with someone. Each slogan in the deck says what it urges, if anything (26 of the 40 do). Text put up from outside is read for words that urge something (`ADVICE_WORDS`: pray, meal, ale, nap, buy, market, sword, work, neighbour, friend and so on, whole words with a plain ending); the first one decides. Advice someone cannot take now is not offered: after dark only going home to rest, and children only being friendly, eating, praying and resting.
- **Who follows it.** Without a model, how they took the board decides the odds: 60% for love, 40% for unease (the ominous slogans urge going home or praying), 25% for a laugh, 15% for a scoff (an advert can win over a sceptic), never when annoyed. The RNG is drawn only when there is advice and a chance. With a model, the prompt says what the board seems to urge and `react_to_billboard` takes an optional `follow`; the model's yes or no wins, and when it leaves `follow` out the odds decide. The model can only accept or decline the advice the sim read from the text, so a notice from outside cannot steer anyone to anything else. Whoever follows it gets the activity queued (`IntentSource::Routine`, so the next block or the model's own plan still takes over) for the middle of its usual length, and their memory of the board ends "I decided to …", which later prompts see.
- **Effects of a reaction:** a speech bubble, an emote (laugh, heart, thinking, anger, alert), a small change to the social need (and energy for love and unease), a low-importance memory quoting the board ("Read the billboard at the market: “…” It made me laugh."), and the model's thought if it gave one. The memories reach later prompts, so villagers can bring it up.

## The pieces

- **World** (both twins, `src/world` and `server/crates/world`): `PlaceDef.billboards` with an id, a local position and a rotation. Each board adds a box obstacle (3.4 × 0.5 m) and two `read_board` slots in front of it, appended after the authored slots so existing slot ids stay the same. Boards: the green, the market, the tavern, the training yard and the west road; the chapel and the Whispering Glade were added later (see below). No place was added, so scatter and the prompts' place list are unchanged.
- **Sim**: `data/billboards.rs` (the deck with its advice, the advice words and the stock lines), `systems/billboards.rs` (what a board shows and urges, cleaning text, the stock reaction, reacting, following advice, wanting to read), the `ReadBoard` activity in `intents.rs` and `routine.rs`, and the two commands in `sim.rs`.
- **Brain**: `RequestKind::Billboard`, `billboard_prompt`, the `react_to_billboard` tool (with `follow`).
- **Server**: `Snapshot.billboards`, `WireEvent::Billboard` (sent by the tracker whenever what a board shows changes, at midnight or after an admin edit), and `POST /api/admin/billboards {id, text?, tone?}` (admins only, JSON, CSRF-checked; text is trimmed, whitespace collapsed, control characters refused, at most 90 UTF-16 units; no text returns the board to the slogans).
- **Client**: `render/billboards.ts` builds each board from three.js boxes and a plane with a canvas texture (no new model, so no asset pipeline run), painted by tone and laid out by `render/billboardText.ts`. The admin panel has a Billboards section, and the help mentions them.

## Consequences

- The board obstacles change the nav grid (a new `worldHash`) and the extra dice rolls change how every world plays out. 66 of the 69 cases in `server/crates/brain/tests/fixtures/prompts.json` now carry the Rust sim's text; their fixed wording (system text, place and activity lists, tool lists) did not change.
- Old saves load unchanged (both new fields are optional and left out when empty).
- No `PROTOCOL_VERSION` bump: older clients ignore the new event and field, and the changed world hash already tells a stale tab to reload.

## Words from the model, and the watchers (October 2026)

- **The model writes the day's words.** A board without an admin's notice waits for the day's words (`wants_words`). While the model has room to spare (`Tier::Idle`, not backlogged), the brain asks for one board per scan (`RequestKind::Slogan`, `slogan_prompt`, the `write_billboard` tool), each board once a day. The prompt gives the board's place, the tone, the chronicle of the last two days (newest eight entries), the villagers' names and what the other boards say today. The answer is `Command::WriteBillboard {boardId, day, text}`; the sim cleans it, caps it like any notice and keeps it in `SimState.billboards` with `day` set, so it comes down at midnight. An admin's notice (no `day`) always wins and stays until taken down. Without a model, or without an answer, the board shows the stock slogan, as before.
- **The tones stay.** The sim decides each board's tone for the day (the stock slogan's tone, from seed, day and board) and the model writes in it, so the mix of uplifting, absurd, pushy, ominous and wholesome is the same with or without a model. What a written notice urges is read from its words, as for an admin's.
- **The watchers.** About one board-day in fourteen (`hints_at_watchers`, from the seed, the day and the board, so nothing is drawn from the RNG) a board hints that someone beyond the world is watching the village, which is the viewers. The model is asked to slip the hint in; without one the board shows a line from `WATCHER_DECK`. Readers of such a board take it in one of three new ways: `shocked` (the timid), `angry` (the hot-headed) or `intrigued` (everyone else), or as the model says. These reactions leave a weightier memory (importance 5, so it comes up in talk), a stock thought when the model gives none, and a line in the chronicle. The sim only accepts these reactions for a board that hints; otherwise they become uneasy, scoff and annoyed. After a couple of such boards a reader settles on what they make of it for good: see [believers and sceptics](2026-10-10-believers-and-sceptics-design.md).
- **Two more boards**, by the chapel and in the Whispering Glade, in both world twins. They change the nav grid (a new `worldHash`), the golden fixtures and how worlds play out.
- **The wire** only gains `slogan` in `RequestKind`, for the decision log. A model-written board is not `custom` (that still means an admin's notice). No `PROTOCOL_VERSION` bump.

## Later

- Letting an admin (or advertiser) say outright what a notice urges, rather than reading it from the words.
- Images on a board (an `imageUrl` next to the text) and an ad feed that sends `SetBillboard` on a schedule.
- Letting the model choose to read a board, if it seems worth the extra prompt text.
